Is Your Team Accidentally Leaking Customer Records?

Safer, GDPR-aware habits for using AI tools without putting unnecessary personal or confidential information into the wrong system.

Is pasting customer information into ChatGPT automatically a GDPR breach?

Not automatically. The legal position depends on the information, the tool, the processing arrangement, your lawful basis, security controls and what you told people. But pasting identifiable customer or confidential business information into an unapproved public tool can create serious compliance and confidentiality risks.

Three safer habits

  1. Minimise: remove names, addresses, emails and identifiers wherever they are not needed.
  2. Use approved tools: understand account settings, retention, processors and organisational controls before handling business data.
  3. Write a simple policy: tell the team which tools are permitted, what information is prohibited and when a human must review the output.

Make the safe action the easy action

Training works best when it uses real examples from the team’s day-to-day work. The aim is not to frighten people away from AI; it is to give them repeatable boundaries that protect customers and the business.

Use placeholders

Try [Client A], [Project value] or [Local address] while drafting. Put sensitive details back only inside an approved workflow.
